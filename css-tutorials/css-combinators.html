Computers

A computer is an advanced electronic device designed to accept raw data as input, process it under the control of a set of instructions called a program, and produce the desired output. At its core, the hardware consists of a Central Processing Unit (CPU), memory modules, and storage drives that work in tandem to execute complex mathematical and logical operations at incredible speeds.

Over the decades, these machines have evolved from massive, room-sized mainframes into highly portable devices like laptops, smartphones, and embedded microcontrollers. This rapid evolution is largely driven by advances in semiconductor technology, which allow billions of microscopic transistors to fit onto a single silicon chip, exponentially increasing computing power while reducing size and energy consumption.

Today, computers serve as the foundational backbone of global infrastructure, powering everything from internet communication and financial markets to medical diagnostics and artificial intelligence. As software continues to advance, the symbiotic relationship between human developers and computing hardware is reshaping how society creates, communicates, and solves complex global challenges.
